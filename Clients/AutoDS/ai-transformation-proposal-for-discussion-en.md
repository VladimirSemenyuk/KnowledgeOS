# AutoDS: AI Transformation Program for the Product and Engineering Organization

Date: 2026-07-19  
Status: Working proposal for discussion

## Executive Summary

The goal of the program is not to introduce isolated AI tools, but to generate a measurable return on investment by changing the entire Product and Engineering system: more validated product outcomes with the same capacity, shorter time to production, less rework and risk, and more disciplined investment in AI.

Vladimir participates as an independent advisor: he leads the methodology, works individually and in groups with the transformation owner and functional leaders, supports key decisions, and validates results. The primary value of this work is the development of leadership maturity and the management system across the entire organization. AutoDS manages the program, delivers the technical implementation, and is accountable for adopting the changes.

## What AutoDS Is Buying

The program is designed to deliver three sequential outcomes:

1. **A controlled start:** a delivery and economic baseline, an ROI model, a clear mandate, and a safe minimum environment for agentic work.
2. **A validated way of working:** repeatable AI-native processes, measured impact in priority value streams, and a decision on what is economically justified to scale.
3. **The internal capability to continue:** permanent owners, an operating model, and a governance system for monitoring impact and future AI investments.

Faster delivery is the mechanism.  
The ultimate outcome is additional product value and a return on AutoDS's total investment.

## What Makes This Format Different

The program is not limited to implementing AI tools or training engineers in new ways of working. Tools accelerate individual operations, but they do not resolve weak product inputs, unclear decision rights, late risk discovery, or poor cross-functional alignment.

Vladimir's primary focus is the leaders and the management system of the Product and Engineering organization:

- the quality of decisions made by the transformation owner and functional leaders;
- clarity of mandates, accountability, and decision rights;
- the ability to manage speed, quality, cost, and risk as one system;
- collaboration across Product, Engineering, QA, Data, and Security;
- the use of metrics and ROI to select, scale, and stop initiatives;
- leadership development through individual work, group sessions, and reviews of real decisions.

The format creates significant value because a more mature management system affects the entire organization rather than a single team or tool. It continues to create impact after the regular advisory engagement ends. Engineer training and tool implementation may be part of AutoDS's implementation, but they are not Vladimir's core offering.

## Expected Impact

This is a target hypothesis. The baseline will be established in Horizon 1; the outcome depends on AutoDS's internal execution, the quality of product inputs, technical investment, and the continued enforcement of quality and risk requirements.

| Period | Expected trajectory |
| --- | --- |
| Months 0–3 | Establish the baseline, remove first-order constraints, run safe experiments, and identify the first local signals. Raw delivery metrics may temporarily plateau or decline while new practices and instrumentation are introduced and processes are redesigned. |
| Months 4–6 | Target **10–20% improvement over the delivery baseline in priority value streams**, without lower quality or increased rework. |
| Months 6–9 | Expand validated practices across most of the organization and continue improving beyond the month-six level. |
| Months 9–12 | Potential for multi-fold gains in selected task categories and mature value streams after scaling, automation, and the establishment of an AI-native working culture. |

Multi-fold gains across the entire organization become realistic first in the value streams where the new practices have demonstrated impact and become a sustainable standard way of working.

## Horizon 1. Remove Constraints

**Objective:** establish the mandate, define the baseline, and create a minimum controlled environment for safe agentic execution.

### Core work

- interviews with the transformation owner and leaders of key functions;
- individual and group work with leaders on mandates, decision quality, and management constraints;
- review of three to five completed features from product intent to production;
- baseline metrics for cycle time, delivery volume, quality, rework, and cost;
- assessment of current AI usage, specifications, context, CI/CD, and QA;
- definition of the outcome metric and success and stop criteria;
- program mandate, decision rights, operating cadence, and escalation process;
- AI usage policy and autonomy levels by risk class;
- minimum requirements for the agentic environment, observability, and quality gates;
- controlled validation of new practices in one or two priority value streams;
- initial economic model and ROI measurement plan.

### Deliverables

- a map of the current feature journey and the main sources of time loss;
- delivery, quality, and economic baselines;
- an ROI model with a list of required data;
- a map of organizational, process, and technical constraints;
- a transformation charter and interim governance system;
- decision rights, accountability, and escalation process agreed across leaders;
- results of the controlled practice validation;
- a backlog and a decision on whether to proceed to Horizon 2.

### Gate

The transformation owner confirms the baseline, mandate, internal resources, and priorities for the next horizon. The success of Horizon 1 is evaluated based on the establishment of a baseline, mandate, safe operating environment, and the data required for a continuation decision—not on mandatory delivery growth. Any material, sustained deterioration beyond agreed limits triggers a separate review and a decision to adjust or stop the program. The program does not continue without an owner, a mandate, and internal execution capacity.

## Horizon 2. Enable AI-Native Processes

**Objective:** turn AI from a local practice used by individual employees into a repeatable way of working across Product, Engineering, QA, Data, and Security.

### Core work

- a specification standard for agreed task categories;
- a Golden Path for common journeys from requirement to production;
- rules for agentic execution and human decisions;
- moving quality controls earlier in the process and updating the Definition of Done;
- shared delivery, quality, cost, and adoption metrics;
- working sessions with leaders on cross-functional decisions, metrics, and scaling constraints;
- controlled scaling of validated practices;
- preparation of internal standard owners and AI Champions.

### Deliverables

- repeatable AI-native processes in priority value streams;
- AI-native PDLC playbook version 1.0;
- the first comparable before-and-after data;
- an updated ROI calculation based on actual data;
- a decision on which practices are economically justified to scale;
- leaders capable of independently assessing the impact, quality, and risks of new practices;
- a plan for the next wave and a transition package for Horizon 3.

### Gate

The transformation owner confirms the practices that have demonstrated impact, the target organization-wide standard, and the organizational decisions that must be institutionalized in the permanent model.

## Horizon 3. Institutionalize the Internal System

**Objective:** establish internal accountability and a management cadence so that the transformation can continue without Vladimir's ongoing presence.

### Core work

- the target operating model for the AI-native PDLC;
- permanent owners of practices, metrics, context, quality, and risk;
- the transformation owner's decision cadence and escalation process;
- individual and group work with leaders until the new management cadence is applied consistently;
- AgentOps and cost controls for agentic execution;
- rules for ownership, versioning, and maintenance of the playbook;
- a development plan for the next 12 months;
- a process for quarterly reviews of ROI and future investments.

### Deliverables

- a functioning internal governance system;
- formal owner mandates;
- independent development and scaling of practices;
- a plan for the next investments and criteria for continuing them;
- reduction of Vladimir's regular involvement to targeted advisory support.

### Completion criteria

The monthly advisory engagement ends when:

- permanent owners have been appointed;
- the management cadence has completed at least two cycles;
- the metrics and escalation process are operational;
- a process for updating the playbook has been defined;
- the transformation owner has approved the plan for the next 12 months.

Two months is the minimum duration of Horizon 3. If the criteria have not been met, the engagement continues on a monthly basis.

## Roles and Responsibilities

### Transformation Owner

AutoDS appoints a leader with the mandate to make cross-functional decisions and resolve priorities and escalations. Michael or Anton may take this role; the choice remains with AutoDS.

### AutoDS

- appoints a program manager with at least 0.75 FTE allocated to the program;
- manages the plan, status, dependencies, risks, and escalations;
- provides access to data and ensures the participation of value-stream owners;
- is accountable for the technical architecture and implementation;
- runs team rituals;
- builds the platform environment, CI/CD, observability, and policies;
- is accountable for internal communication, training, and adoption of practices.

The transformation owner and program manager may be different people: the former makes key decisions, while the latter manages day-to-day coordination.

## Economic Model and ROI

The AI transformation pays for itself when the change in ways of working translates into one or more validated outcomes:

- earlier product outcomes and lower cost of delay;
- additional realized capacity without proportional headcount growth;
- less rework and fewer defects and incidents;
- avoided or deferred hiring;
- lower spending on duplicative tools and ineffective AI pilots.

### Formula

```text
Total investment
= Vladimir's fees
+ internal participants' time
+ technical implementation, models, tools, and infrastructure

12-month ROI
= (annual economic impact − total investment) ÷ total investment

Payback period
= total investment ÷ (annual economic impact ÷ 12)
```

The minimum program fee is $100,000. AutoDS's total investment will be higher and will be refined in Horizon 1. A range of $145,000–$175,000 is used for the preliminary stress test; it is not a final budget estimate.

### Break-Even Point

The table shows the share of the Product and Engineering organization's annual cost that must be converted into realized impact—additional delivery—to recover the investment.

| Total annual cost of the organization | To recover the $100,000 fee | To recover the total investment of $145,000–$175,000 |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $2m | 5.0% | 7.3–8.8% |
| $3m | 3.3% | 4.8–5.8% |
| $5m | 2.0% | 2.9–3.5% |
| $8m | 1.25% | 1.8–2.2% |

This is not an assumption about AutoDS's costs. The actual calculation requires company data.

### Illustrative Scenario for the 80 → 90 Target

An increase from 80 to 90 stories per sprint represents a 12.5% improvement. Because stories are not a financial metric, the conservative scenario assumes that only 50% of the improvement translates into additional validated product outcomes. The realized improvement is therefore 6.25%.

| Annual cost of the organization | Annual impact at 6.25% | ROI on the total investment of $145,000–$175,000 | Payback period |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $2m | $125,000 | −29% to −14% | 13.9–16.8 months |
| $3m | $187,500 | 7% to 29% | 9.3–11.2 months |
| $5m | $312,500 | 79% to 116% | 5.6–6.7 months |
| $8m | $500,000 | 186% to 245% | 3.5–4.2 months |

The scenario demonstrates how sensitive ROI is to the organization's cost base and its ability to convert technical improvement into product outcomes. It does not include the value of earlier feature releases, reduced rework, avoided incidents, or optimized AI spending.

### Data Required for the Actual Calculation

Horizon 1 must confirm:

- headcount and the total annual cost of the organization;
- internal time and technical program costs;
- the current hiring budget and time to hire;
- cycle time, rework, defects, incidents, and review time;
- the cost of delay for a priority feature;
- current spending on AI tools, models, infrastructure, and pilots.

The transformation owner will then receive `low / base / high` scenarios, monthly update rules, and criteria for stopping investments.

## Commercial Terms

- Horizons 1 and 2 have a fixed fee of $40,000 each.
- For Horizons 1 and 2, 50% is paid before the start and 50% at the midpoint of the horizon.
- Horizon 3 is paid at $10,000 per month in advance, with a minimum commitment of two months.
- After the minimum term, Horizon 3 continues month to month until the completion criteria are met.
- Additional involvement beyond the agreed scope is billed at $3,500 per day or $400 per hour for short consultations.
- Scope changes, external expenses, and technical implementation are agreed separately.
- If Horizon 1 or 2 cannot be completed within two weeks after its baseline end date for reasons attributable to AutoDS, the parties may choose one of the following options once per horizon: accept the deliverables based on the available data; pause the work and agree on one new completion date, subject to Vladimir's availability; or continue the horizon for $10,000 per additional calendar month.
- If the new date after a pause is also missed for reasons attributable to AutoDS, Vladimir's further involvement is available only as a paid continuation at $10,000 per month; no second free pause is provided.

### Early Achievement of Outcomes

- Early improvement in an individual metric redirects the remaining work toward validating sustainability, quality, ROI, and scalability.
- If all criteria for a horizon are met early, the parties may accept it ahead of schedule and begin the next horizon earlier.
- The fixed fee for a completed horizon is not reduced: AutoDS is buying an outcome, methodology, and reserved availability—not hours used.
- Unused calendar time does not become a bank of hours and cannot be transferred to work outside the agreed scope.
- Horizon 3 may begin earlier, but it lasts for at least two months so that the internal system can be validated over a minimum of two cycles.
- A shorter overall program schedule does not reduce the minimum fee of $100,000.

If AutoDS purchases the horizons separately, it may stop after paying for and accepting a completed horizon. The outcomes of subsequent horizons are not included in Vladimir's obligations in that case.

## Excluded from the Base Fee

- day-to-day project management and status preparation;
- technical implementation and management of external vendors;
- specialized Data Science, platform, security, or compliance expertise;
- licenses, models, tokens, cloud infrastructure, and data preparation;
- travel and external expenses;
- legal, regulatory, or certification expertise.
